# Manage costs by using alerts, budgets, and Azure Advisor recommendations

## Key concepts

- A budget in Cost Management plans spending and holds people accountable; hitting a threshold only sends notifications — resources are not affected and consumption is not stopped. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Cost Management has three alert types: budget alerts, credit alerts, and department spending quota alerts; a separate cost anomaly alert can also be created for unexpected charges. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Budget alerts fire when usage or cost reaches or exceeds the amount in the budget's alert condition; in the portal budgets are defined by cost, while the Azure Consumption API allows cost-based or usage-based budgets. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Credit alerts apply to Azure Prepayment (previously called monetary commitment) on Enterprise Agreements and are generated automatically at 90% and 100% of the credit balance, emailed to account owners. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Department spending quota alerts fire when a department's spending reaches a fixed quota threshold set in the portal (for example 50% or 75%), emailing department owners. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Advisor is a digital cloud assistant that analyzes resource configuration and usage telemetry and recommends changes to improve cost effectiveness, performance, reliability, and security. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-overview))
- Advisor recommendations are split into five categories: Reliability, Security, Performance, Cost, and Operational excellence. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-overview))
- Advisor cost recommendations for underutilized virtual machines and VM scale sets propose shutting down or resizing, selected by machine-learning models, and display the estimated savings for either action. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))

## Settings and limits

- Budget scopes: Azure RBAC scopes (management group, subscription, resource group); EA scopes (billing account, department, enrollment account); individual agreement billing account; Microsoft Customer Agreement scopes (billing account, billing profile, invoice section, customer). ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- For an MCA billing account, evaluation always uses USD regardless of billing currency; billing profile, invoice section and customer are evaluated in billing currency. In sovereign clouds such as China 21V, evaluation uses billing currency instead of USD. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Reset periods are Monthly, Quarterly, Annually (calendar-aligned) or Billing month, Billing quarter, Billing year (invoice-aligned, for pay-as-you-go, MSDN and Visual Studio subscriptions and for resource groups). ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Evaluated cost restarts at zero each new period: a quarterly amount is divided evenly across three months, an annual amount across the 12 calendar months. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Setting an expiration date in the future makes the budget reset automatically at the end of each period with the same amount, and the budget is deleted when it expires; differing future amounts therefore need separate budgets. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- A budget needs at least one cost threshold (% of budget) and one email address; you may add up to five thresholds and five email addresses, and alert limits span 0.01% to 1000% of the budget threshold. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- The Type field toggles between Actual and Forecasted alerts; forecasted alerts warn in advance from predicted costs. Add azure-noreply@microsoft.com to approved senders so alert mail is not junked. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Action groups can be attached with Manage action group when creating or editing a budget, but only subscription and resource group scopes support them (the Azure mobile app covers those same two scopes only). ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Budget permissions: Owner can create, modify or delete; Contributor and Cost Management Contributor can create, modify and delete their own budgets and change amounts of budgets created by others; Reader and Cost Management Reader can only view. EA needs read access to view and contributor permission to create and manage. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Budget evaluation uses actual cost and does not include amortization, and now includes reserved instance and purchase data; filter Publisher Type = Azure and Charge Type = Usage to evaluate first-party Azure consumption only. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Every subscription in the scope must use a single currency; multi-currency evaluation is unsupported and alerts can be missed. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Cost and usage data is typically available within 8–24 hours, budgets are evaluated every 24 hours, and threshold emails normally arrive within an hour of that evaluation; a brand-new subscription can take up to 48 hours before Cost Management features work. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Advisor recommendations can be read by Owner, Contributor or Reader at subscription, resource group or resource scope, and Advisor also surfaces Microsoft Defender for Cloud recommendations. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-overview))
- Advisor VM/VMSS lookback period is configurable to 7, 14, 21, 30, 60 or 90 days; changes take up to 48 hours to reach recommendations, while a per-subscription average CPU utilization filter takes up to 24 hours. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- Shutdown recommendation criteria: P95 of maximum CPU utilization summed across all cores under 3%, P100 of average CPU over the last 3 days (summed across cores) at or below 2%, and outbound network utilization under 2% over seven days; memory is not considered. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- Resize targets leave headroom on the recommended SKU: user-facing workloads at or below 40% for P95 CPU plus outbound network and 60% for P99 memory; non user-facing workloads 80% for both. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- Burstable (B-series) advice requires average CPU below the baseline, P95 of CPU below twice the baseline, no accelerated networking on the current SKU, and enough burstable credits for average CPU over seven days; the suggested SKU keeps the same core count. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- Metrics are sampled every 30 seconds, aggregated to 1 minute, then to 30 minutes taking the maximum of averages; VMSS metrics are averaged across instances for instance-count advice and maximized for SKU-change advice, and instance-count recommendations outrank SKU changes because they are easier to act on. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))

## Compare and choose

- Alert type by offer: budget alerts work on Enterprise Agreement, Microsoft Customer Agreement and web direct/Pay-As-You-Go; credit alerts and department spending quota alerts are Enterprise Agreement only. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Alert permissions: Owner, Contributor and Cost Management Contributor get Read and Update; Reader and Cost Management Reader get Read only, across all Azure RBAC scopes. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Choose an Actual alert to react when accrued cost reaches the threshold; choose a Forecasted alert for early warning that projected spend will exceed it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Use a budget to track spend against a limit and Advisor cost recommendations to optimize configuration; budget alert details link to cost analysis and to the recommendation when one exists. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Advisor recommendation states are active, postponed (hide for now), dismissed (not relevant) and completed (you took the action); manually completed items show as "Marked completed" until Azure Advisor verifies and completes them. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-overview))
- A resize can target a smaller SKU in the same family, a newer version, or a different family (for example D3v2 to D2v2, D3v2 to D2v3, or D3v2 to E3v2); the target must keep accelerated networking and premium storage capabilities and be supported in the VM's current region. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))

## Gotchas

- Advisor's savings figures use retail rates and ignore account discounts and Reserved Instance / Savings plan purchases, so real savings may be lower; for cross-series recommendations, following the advice can even increase costs when RIs exist. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- Budgets created with PowerShell do not send notifications; REST APIs are recommended over CLI, and MCA customers should use the Budgets REST API. Assigning alert recipient roles works only through the Budgets API, not the portal. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
- Right-sizing advice ignores anything beyond CPU, memory and outbound network, and often does not apply to VMs staged for upcoming traffic, DR copies, homogeneity requirements, or SKUs under specific testing — use Dismiss or Postpone there. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations))
- After implementing a recommendation, Advisor can take up to a day to register the change. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-overview))
- Alerts show status active or dismissed, and a dismissed alert can be reactivated manually; dismissed recommendations likewise return to active until system verification. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/cost-mgt-alerts-monitor-usage-spending))
- Budget evaluations now include reserved instance and purchase data, which can trigger new alerts even though billed charges did not change — re-check thresholds. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets))
