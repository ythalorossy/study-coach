# Configure management groups

## Key concepts

- A management group is a governance scope that sits above subscriptions, so access, policy, and compliance conditions cascade by inheritance to every nested management group, subscription, resource group, and resource beneath it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Because assignments inherit, one role assignment or policy at a management group can replace many per-subscription assignments; for example a role like **VM Contributor** has no action on the management group itself but flows down to all VMs below it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Every Microsoft Entra directory has exactly one top-level container, the root management group, and all subscriptions and management groups in that tenant fold up into it; new subscriptions are automatically placed there on creation. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- The root management group's default display name is **Tenant root group**, its ID equals the Microsoft Entra tenant ID, and it behaves like any other management group — but it can never be moved or deleted. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Anyone who has access to a subscription can see where it sits in the hierarchy, but nobody has default access to the root management group; only Microsoft Entra **Global Administrators** can elevate access to it, and initial elevation grants the **User Access Administrator** role on the root group. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Anything assigned at the root management group (user access or policy) applies to every resource in the whole directory, so assignments there should be treated as "must have" only. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Only management groups and subscriptions can be children of a management group; each management group and subscription has exactly one parent, each management group can have many children, and a directory holds a single hierarchy. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage)) ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Every subscription grouped under the same management group has to trust the one Microsoft Entra tenant, and cost management features don't cover management groups for Microsoft Customer Agreement (MCA) subscriptions today. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- The first management group created in a directory triggers one-time service setup and can take up to 15 minutes; you get a notification when it finishes. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/create-management-group-portal))
- With hierarchy protection switched off, no explicit write permission is required to create a group: the new management group lands under the root management group or the configured default management group, and the person who made it is granted **Owner** on it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/create-management-group-portal))
- Management group events, including role assignment and policy assignment changes, can be audited through Azure Monitor activity logs, and diagnostic settings on a management group can stream those logs to a Log Analytics workspace, Azure Storage, or Azure Event Hubs. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage)) ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))

## Settings and limits

- A single directory can contain up to 10,000 management groups, and a management group tree supports a maximum of six levels of depth — that count excludes the root level and the subscription level. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- When creating a group in the portal (path **All services** > **Management + governance** > **Management Groups** > **+ Add management group**), the **Management Group ID** is the directory-unique identifier used to submit commands and cannot be edited after creation, while the display name shown in the portal is optional and can be changed at any time. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/create-management-group-portal))
- Changing the display name: in the portal use **Rename Group**, in PowerShell `Update-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'ContosoIt' -DisplayName 'Contoso Group'`, and in the CLI `az account management-group update --name 'Contoso' --display-name 'Contoso Group'`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- In PowerShell, `-GroupId` is an alias of `-GroupName` for `AzManagementGroup` cmdlets, so either can carry the management group ID as a string. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Deleting a management group requires that it has no child management groups or subscriptions and that you hold write permission — **Owner**, **Contributor**, or **Management Group Contributor**; check your effective permissions on the group's **IAM** blade, and hover over a greyed-out **Delete** button to see the blocking reason. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Delete commands are `Remove-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso'` and `az account management-group delete --name 'Contoso'`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- You can view any group where you have a direct or inherited role: `Get-AzManagementGroup`, `Get-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso'`, and `Get-AzManagementGroup -GroupId TestGroupParent -Expand -Recurse` for the whole subtree, or `az account management-group list`, `az account management-group show --name 'Contoso'`, and `az account management-group show --name 'Contoso' -e -r`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Adding an existing subscription in the portal uses **Add subscription** on the parent group's page; removing it uses the row's `...` menu > **Move**, then supplying **New parent management group ID**. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Subscription link commands are `New-AzManagementGroupSubscription -GroupId 'Contoso' -SubscriptionId '12345678-1234-1234-1234-123456789012'` and `Remove-AzManagementGroupSubscription` with the same parameters, or `az account management-group subscription add|remove --name 'Contoso' --subscription '...'`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Moving a management group under a different parent is done with `Update-AzManagementGroup -GroupId 'Contoso' -ParentId $parentGroup.id`, or `az account management-group update --name 'Contoso' --parent ContosoIT`, or in the portal with **Create** > **Create new** or **Create** > **Use existing** on the new parent. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- To move a subscription declaratively, deploy at tenant level a resource of type `Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/subscriptions` with API version `2020-05-01`, named `<targetMgId>/<subscriptionId>` at scope `/`; the Bicep equivalent uses `targetScope = 'managementGroup'` with the same resource type at `scope: tenant()`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- The canonical scope path for a management group — used in role assignments, policy definitions, activity log queries, PowerShell, CLI, and REST — is `/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/{yourMgID}`, e.g. `New-AzRoleAssignment -Scope "/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/Contoso"`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- A management group can be an assignable scope in an Azure custom role definition; use its ID, not its display name, in `/providers/Microsoft.management/managementgroups/{groupId}`, because ARM does not validate that the referenced management group actually exists. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Custom role limits on management groups: only one management group may appear in the assignable scopes of a new role, and custom roles with `DataActions` can't be assigned at management group scope. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Moving requires write permission at three layers — on the child being moved (`Microsoft.management/managementgroups/write`, plus `Microsoft.management/managementgroups/subscriptions/write` for subscriptions, `Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/write`, `Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/delete`, and `Microsoft.Management/register/action`) and `Microsoft.management/managementgroups/write` on both the target and the current parent. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))

## Compare and choose

- For grouping, use a management group when you need inherited role and policy assignments from an assignment on the scope to every member and descendant; resource groups and subscriptions also inherit, while **service groups** and tags do not. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Use a **service group** when you need to group shared resources that cross subscription or resource group boundaries (for example global networking shared by many apps) — management groups, subscriptions, resource groups, and tags cannot do this. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Tags can act as criteria inside Azure Policy, and they can be applied across scopes, but they are added resource by resource and are not a first-class grouping — use a management group for consolidation that reduces role and policy assignments. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Role capability matrix on a management group: **Owner** can create, rename, move, delete, assign access, assign policy, and read; **Contributor** and **Management Group Contributor** can create, rename, move, delete, and read but cannot assign access or policy; **Reader** and **Management Group Reader** can only read; **Resource Policy Contributor** can only assign policy; **User Access Administrator** can only assign access and assign policy. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- **Management Group Contributor** and **Management Group Reader** act only on the management group scope itself, while built-in resource roles like **Contributor** and **Reader** also apply to everything beneath the group. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- **Management Group Contributor** is the least-privilege built-in role that can create, rename, move, and delete management groups; **Contributor** gives the same management group actions but also grants write access to all descendant resources. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- For granting people access to many subscriptions at once, assign the role once on the management group so it inherits down, instead of scripting role assignments on each subscription. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- For granting people rights to change role assignments on a management group use **User Access Administrator**; for changing policy assignments use **Resource Policy Contributor**; **Owner** is the only built-in role that covers create, rename, move, delete, assign access, and assign policy. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- To attach an existing subscription use **Add subscription**; to detach it from a parent, use the subscription's **Move** pane with a **New parent management group ID** or `Remove-AzManagementGroupSubscription`, which unlinks without deleting anything. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Use the portal for interactive one-off moves, PowerShell or CLI (`add`, `remove`, `update --parent`) for scripted moves, and the tenant-level ARM template or Bicep file for subscriptions so the same move runs as code in pipelines. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))

## Gotchas

- Azure Resource Manager caches the management group hierarchy and user tokens for up to 30 minutes, so a move or rename may not show up in the portal right away; refresh the browser, sign out and back in, or request a new token to see it sooner. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- There's an exception to move permissions: if the target or the current parent is the root management group, no permissions are required on it, because it is the default landing spot for every new group and subscription. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- If a subscription's **Owner** role is inherited from its current management group, you can only move it to a management group where you also hold **Owner** — moving to one where you're only **Contributor** would strip your ownership. If **Owner** is assigned directly on the subscription, **Contributor** at the target is enough. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage))
- Moving a subscription whose role assignment points at a custom role defined on a different branch is blocked, because the path from assignment to role definition would break. Fixes: delete the role assignment first, add the subscription to the role's assignable scope, widen the assignable scope (for example up to the root), or define another custom role in the destination branch. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- A common mistake is putting the display name in a custom role's `AssignableScopes`; the management group **ID** must be used, and a wrong ID is accepted silently because ARM doesn't validate the group exists. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Renaming the root management group's display name requires **Owner** or **Contributor** on the root group itself, and since nobody has default access there you must first elevate as a Microsoft Entra **Global Administrator**. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- Deleting the root management group is impossible by design, so never put disposable groups there; conversely, a group with any child management group or subscription can't be deleted until those children are moved out. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/manage)) ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview))
- The first-ever management group in a fresh directory may appear to hang for about 15 minutes while the service is provisioned for that tenant; wait for the completion notification instead of retrying the creation. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/create-management-group-portal))
- Group creation isn't gated by role assignments when hierarchy protection is off, so unexpected groups can appear and each creator ends up as their **Owner** — verify that hierarchy protection and a default management group are configured. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/create-management-group-portal))
