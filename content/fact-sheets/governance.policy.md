# Implement and manage Azure Policy

## Key concepts

- A policy definition is the business rule: it compares resource properties and names the `effect` to apply. Its JSON carries `displayName`, `description`, `mode`, `version`, `metadata`, `parameters` and `policyRule`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `policyRule` has an `if` clause testing resource `field`s (reached through aliases) or literal `value`s, plus a `then` clause holding the `effect`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- An initiative definition (called `PolicySet` by the CLI and Azure PowerShell) groups policy definitions under one goal and is assigned as a single unit. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- An assignment binds a definition or initiative to a scope — management group, subscription, resource group or one resource — and child scopes inherit it unless excluded. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Four evaluation triggers: resource create or update in an assigned scope, a new assignment on a scope, an update to an already-assigned definition or initiative, and the routine cycle that runs once every 24 hours. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Effects set in the policy rule pick the response: deny the change, log it, modify the resource before or after the change, deploy related compliant resources, or block actions on resources. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Identity-aware rules inspect `requestContext().identity` to act on who is calling, for example requiring multifactor authentication or stopping manual deletion of critical resources. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Policy judges resource state and actions; Azure RBAC judges who may act. RBAC permission never rescues a Policy denial, and the combination gives full scope control. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Policy operations sit in `Microsoft.Authorization` and `Microsoft.PolicyInsights`. Resource Policy Contributor holds most of them, Owner has all, Contributor and Reader get read only — and Contributor may trigger remediation but cannot create or update definitions and assignments. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Definitions, initiatives and assignments are readable by every role holder at the assignment's scope and below. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- The managed identity on a `deployIfNotExists` or `modify` assignment needs rights to create or update the targeted resources, and User Access Administrator grants them. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Even when assigned at management group level, only resources at subscription or resource group level are evaluated. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Azure Arc brings non-Azure servers and Kubernetes into Policy evaluation, and all Policy data and objects are encrypted at rest. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))

## Settings and limits

- `displayName` caps at 128 characters, `description` at 512, and each `metadata` property at 1,024; common metadata keys are `version`, `category`, `preview`, `deprecated` and `portalReview`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `policyType` is read-only: `Builtin` (Microsoft maintained), `Custom` (anything you author), `Static` (a Regulatory Compliance definition owned by Microsoft, sometimes shown as **Microsoft managed**). ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `mode` `all` evaluates resource groups, subscriptions and all resource types; `indexed` evaluates only resource types that support tags and location. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Use `mode` `all` for resource groups and subscriptions by targeting `Microsoft.Resources/subscriptions/resourceGroups` or `Microsoft.Resources/subscriptions`, and it is the recommendation (and the portal default) for most definitions. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- An omitted `mode` defaults to `all` in Azure PowerShell but to `null` in Azure CLI, and `null` behaves like `indexed` for backward compatibility. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Supported Resource Provider modes: `Microsoft.Kubernetes.Data` (AKS and Arc-enabled Kubernetes; only `audit`, `deny` and `disabled` effects), `Microsoft.KeyVault.Data`, and `Microsoft.Network.Data` for Virtual Network Manager membership policies. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Preview modes: `Microsoft.ManagedHSM.Data`, `Microsoft.DataFactory.Data` (enforcement only, no compliance reported), `Microsoft.MachineLearningServices.v2.Data` (compliance records for new or updated components kept 24 hours, nothing for pre-existing model deployments) and `Microsoft.LoadTestService.Data`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `Microsoft.DataFactory.Data`, `Microsoft.MachineLearningServices.v2.Data`, `Microsoft.ManagedHSM.Data` and `Microsoft.CognitiveServices.Data` have no built-in versioning, and no Resource Provider mode supports custom versioning during preview; such modes normally accept built-in definitions only, with no component-level exemptions. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Versions share one `definitionId` and the latest is used unless you name one; the format is `{Major}.{Minor}.{Patch}` — major for breaking rule changes, minor for new parameters, allowed values or `roleDefinitionIds` changes, patch for string or metadata edits. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `preview` is a boolean rendered as a `_preview` suffix on the version, deprecation is `"deprecated": "true"` in metadata, and custom versioning (up to four versions per definition or initiative) is still in preview. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Definition location must be a management group or subscription; a subscription location limits assignments to that subscription, while a management group location reaches child management groups and child subscriptions. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Per-scope ceilings: 500 policy definitions, 200 initiative definitions, 200 policy or initiative assignments and 1,000 exemptions; tenant-wide the limit is 2,500 initiative definitions. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Per-object ceilings: 20 parameters per policy definition, 1,000 policies and 400 parameters per initiative definition, 400 exclusion (`notScopes`) entries per assignment, 512 nested conditionals per policy rule, 50,000 resources per remediation task and 1,048,576 bytes per request body. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- In the assign wizard, **Version** auto-fills to the latest major version and auto-injects non-breaking changes, **Assignment name** defaults to the definition name, and **Assigned by** comes from the signed-in account. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- **Policy enforcement** set to *Enabled* enforces the assignment; *Disabled* lets you test the outcome without triggering the effect. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- For a `modify` definition **Create a Managed Identity** is pre-checked, **Type of Managed Identity** defaults to *System Assigned* and **Permissions** to *Contributor*; the box must be checked for any assignment containing `deployIfNotExists` or `modify`. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- **Exclusions** are optional and begin one level below the scope you picked; **Create a remediation task** is a separate option that alters existing non-compliant resources, not just future ones. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- A custom **Non-compliance message** is shown when a request is denied and for non-compliant resources during regular evaluation. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Initiative parameter value types are *Default value*, *Set value* and *Use Initiative Parameter*; a parameter scope picked for unresolved `strongType` values affects authoring only, never evaluation or assignment scope. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Definitions can be authored in the portal (Definitions under Authoring), with `New-AzPolicyDefinition` and `New-AzPolicySetDefinition`, with `az policy definition create` and `az policy set-definition create`, or with a REST `PUT` against the `policydefinitions` endpoint. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))

## Compare and choose

- One standalone rule or per-policy reporting → a single policy definition; several rules sharing one goal → an initiative, so later policies need no extra assignment. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- If one policy must be judged on its own, keep it outside the initiative, because assigning an initiative evaluates every policy inside it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Roll out with `audit` or `auditIfNotExists`, then move to `deny`, `modify` or `deployIfNotExists` once impact is known — enforcement effects can break existing automation such as autoscale. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Create definitions at management group or subscription level and assign at the child subscription or resource group so one definition is reusable. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- To loosen a parent assignment, exclude the child scope: Policy is an explicit-deny system, so a more permissive assignment can never override a denial from another assignment. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- Exclusion (on the assignment, covering a subscription, resource group or single resource) versus policy exemption (skips evaluation for named resources). ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Default to `mode` `all`; use `indexed` only for tag or location enforcement, and Resource Provider modes only for Kubernetes, Key Vault, Managed HSM, Data Factory, Machine Learning, Load Testing or Virtual Network Manager (`Microsoft.Network.Data`) control. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- `modify` and `deployIfNotExists` assignments need a managed identity; `audit`, `auditIfNotExists` and `deny` do not. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Decide with RBAC when the rule depends on the person or group, and with Policy when it depends on the resulting resource state. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))

## Gotchas

- `id`, `type` and `name` are defined outside the JSON body; SDK reads return them but they are read-only and must not be authored. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/definition-structure-basics))
- Assignments always evaluate the latest state of their definition, so editing a definition silently changes every existing assignment of it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
- An initiative's version value is metadata only — the Policy service uses it for no update or processing. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Initiative parameters cannot be deleted once the initiative is saved; remove them from the policy definitions that consume them instead. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Adding one definition twice to an initiative requires a distinct reference ID per instance, which is how each copy gets different parameter values. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- A denied deployment lists the resource as *Forbidden* under the resource group's **Deployments**; the failure details expose the responsible policy object GUIDs, and the initiative's Compliance **Events** tab shows who attempted it. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Right after assignment, Compliance state is usually **Not started** before it becomes **Compliant** once evaluation finishes. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- A definition saved at a subscription can never cover another subscription later — put it in a management group containing all target subscriptions, and the same rule applies to initiatives. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- With a `deny` effect, excluding a single resource that already exists makes little sense; exclude the containing resource group or subscription. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/tutorials/create-and-manage))
- Editing Azure Virtual Network Manager dynamic groups requires an Azure RBAC role assignment carrying `Microsoft.Network/networkManagers/networkGroups/join/action`; classic Administrator or Co-Administrator alone fails. ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview))
